Business plan · Siliguri, then North Bengal and Sikkim

Siliguri Cookie Business Plan

An asset-light cookie brand that the owner governs but does not run. The company owns the brand, the recipes, the systems and the customer data. One lean central kitchen in each city makes all the dough, small company kiosks bake and sell it, and from year two franchisees pay for new outlets. Growth comes from repeating a proven unit, not from the owner's time or money.

Product: a 75 g cookie at ₹60. All figures are planning estimates for 2026 Siliguri prices, before income tax. This plan replaces the owned-kitchen model in the Growth Plan; daily store routines stay as in the Operations Playbook.

The plan on one page

  1. Own what protects the product, rent the rest. The brand, recipes, systems, customer list and one central dough kitchen per city are ours. Shop space, kiosk ovens and franchise outlets are rented or paid for by others.
  2. Prove one unit first. One company kiosk in Siliguri, with delivery apps and WhatsApp orders, months 0 to 6.
  3. Repeat it from profits. Three kiosks in different formats plus festive and corporate gifting, months 6 to 18. Hire an operations head so the owner steps back.
  4. Franchise once the numbers are proven. 15 franchise kiosks across North Bengal and Sikkim, months 18 to 36. Franchisees invest; we earn royalty and a margin on dough.
  5. The owner chairs, the operations head runs. The owner keeps the bank, approvals above ₹50,000, leases, leaders and strategy. Everything else runs on written systems and a weekly dashboard.

Business model

The company is a brand-and-systems business. It makes money from the gap between what customers pay and what partners charge, and later from royalties. Each part of the work goes to whoever already owns the asset it needs.

The trade-off

A kitchen has fixed costs, so it is expensive while a city sells little. Siliguri's starter kitchen costs about ₹49,500 a month, which is ₹6.60 per cookie at 250 a day, more than the roughly ₹4 a contract bakery would charge. Above about 400 cookies a day the kitchen becomes cheaper than outsourcing, and at Phase 3 volumes it costs about ₹2 per cookie. In return you control the recipe, the quality and the supply from day one, and every franchise in the city buys its dough from you.

Unit economics

The business has three money-making units, all supplied by the city kitchen. Kitchen costs are counted separately, in the financial plan, because one kitchen serves every unit in its city. Each is small and repeatable. Growth means adding more of whichever unit earns best.

How each number is built

  • Company kiosk: ₹60 less 5% GST is ₹57.14. Less ₹23.50 cost (ingredients ₹19.50, packaging ₹4) grossed up for 8% waste, and 3% payment fees, leaves ₹29.80. Monthly costs: rent ₹25,000, power ₹6,000, two salespeople ₹24,000 (legal minimum plus Puja bonus), rented oven and freezer ₹5,000, billing and CCTV ₹2,500, licences ₹1,500, marketing ₹15,000, repairs ₹3,000. Dough delivery is a kitchen cost.
  • Gift boxes: ₹330 a box of 6 (₹55 a cookie) less GST, cookie cost, ₹8 box, 1% fees and 5% commission. No waste, because boxes are made to order.
  • Franchise kiosk, our side: 6% royalty on sales after GST (₹3.43 a cookie) plus ₹6.50 margin on each dough and packaging kit we supply at ₹30 and make for ₹23.50.

The franchisee's side

A franchise only sells if it pays the franchisee well. They invest ₹6–8 lakh including a ₹2.5 lakh fee. After paying ₹30 a kit, 6% royalty and 2% to the marketing fund, they keep ₹18.16 a cookie. With costs of ₹75,000 a month, they break even at 138 cookies a day, make about ₹45,000 a month at 220 a day and ₹61,000 at 250, and earn back their investment in 11 to 16 months. That is the pitch, and it has to be proven at company kiosks first.

Phase 4
Years 3–5
Region
Guwahati and the Northeast, each new city opening its own kitchen when it reaches the trigger below, or through a master franchisee who builds and runs the city kitchen to our standards. Online gift boxes across India. The owner's job is now choosing partners and protecting the brand.

City kitchens

Each city has one central kitchen that mixes, scoops and freezes all dough for every outlet in the city and nearby towns. Kiosks only bake. Frozen dough keeps for weeks, so towns up to about 4 hours away can be supplied two or three times a week instead of daily.

The kitchen space

  • 300–600 sq ft on a back street, never on the high street
  • Separate areas: dry store, cold store, mixing, scooping, freezing, packing and dispatch, washing
  • Water, drainage, and a strong 3-phase power supply
  • Inverter or generator for the freezers
  • CCTV on the mixing table and the store room

Equipment

  • 20-litre planetary mixer (a 40-litre one at about 2,000 balls a day)
  • 2 chest freezers, more as volume grows
  • Steel tables, scales, scoops, trays, labelled crates
  • Insulated boxes and ice packs for delivery
  • Bought on an equipment loan, so cash goes to outlets

Keeping every city's cookie the same

  1. One premix. Siliguri mixes the dry ingredients and flavourings and ships sealed bags to other city kitchens, which add only butter, eggs and chocolate. The full recipe stays in one place.
  2. Named brands. Every kitchen buys the same butter and chocolate brands; the company buys the signature chocolate centrally.
  3. Batch records. Each tray is labelled with flavour, date and batch code, and a sample from every batch is kept for 7 days.
  4. Every dough ball counted. For each outlet, dough sent must equal cookies sold plus waste plus stock left, checked weekly from the billing app.
  5. Two days of stock at every outlet, so a power cut or a missed delivery never closes a kiosk.

Each kitchen needs its own FSSAI licence as a food manufacturing unit and a trade licence for its address. Confirm the licence category with your CA before signing the lease.

Outlet operations

Every kiosk, company or franchise, runs the same routine from the Operations Playbook: opening and closing checklists, hourly bake plan, waste logging, one UPI QR on the bill, blind cash count. A board at the counter shows the time of the next fresh batch.

Technology

  • Billing app with multi-outlet and stock (for example Petpooja): every company and franchise outlet must use it. Royalty is calculated from it, and the dough order is generated from it.
  • Simple CRM (Zoho Bigin or a shared Google Sheet) for corporate gifting and franchise leads.
  • WhatsApp Business for orders, the broadcast list and alerts.
  • Payroll and attendance app (PagarBook, Kredily) with selfie attendance at the outlet.
  • Accounting (Zoho Books or Tally), kept by the CA.
  • One dashboard (Google Looker Studio, free) showing every outlet's sales, waste, rating and dough gap, sent to the owner every Monday.

Who makes a good franchisee

A local investor with ₹8–10 lakh available, who lives in the town, will be at the outlet daily or employ a full-time manager, and has run a shop or food business before. Turn down investors who want a passive investment with no one on site.

Process

  1. Enquiry form and a 20-minute call.
  2. Discovery day at a company kiosk: work a shift.
  3. Site proposed by the franchisee, checked against our site rules.
  4. Agreement signed, fee paid.
  5. 30 days of setup with the company's fit-out vendors; 7 days of training.
  6. Launch with the company's launch kit and team for the first week.

India has no single franchise law, so the agreement does all the work. Have a franchise lawyer draft it and register the trademark before signing the first franchisee.
