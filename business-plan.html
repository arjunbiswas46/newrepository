Business plan · Siliguri, then North Bengal and Sikkim

Siliguri Cookie Business Plan

An asset-light cookie brand that the owner governs but does not run. The company owns the brand, the recipes, the systems and the customer data. A licensed bakery makes the dough, small kiosks and partner cafés sell it, and from year two franchisees pay for new outlets. Growth comes from repeating a proven unit, not from the owner's time or money.

Product: a 75 g cookie at ₹60. All figures are planning estimates for 2026 Siliguri prices, before income tax. This plan replaces the owned-kitchen model in the Growth Plan; daily store routines stay as in the Operations Playbook.

The plan on one page

  1. Own what is scarce, rent what is common. The brand, recipes, systems and customer list are ours. Ovens, kitchens, shop space and outlet capital belong to partners.
  2. Prove one unit first. One company kiosk and 10 café partners in Siliguri, months 0 to 6.
  3. Repeat it from profits. Three kiosks in different formats, 40 partners and festive gifting, months 6 to 18. Hire an operations head so the owner steps back.
  4. Franchise once the numbers are proven. 15 franchise kiosks across North Bengal and Sikkim, months 18 to 36. Franchisees invest; we earn royalty and a margin on dough.
  5. The owner chairs, the operations head runs. The owner keeps the bank, approvals above ₹50,000, leases, leaders and strategy. Everything else runs on written systems and a weekly dashboard.

Business model

The company is a brand-and-systems business. It makes money from the gap between what customers pay and what partners charge, and later from royalties. Each part of the work goes to whoever already owns the asset it needs.

The trade-off

Renting production costs margin. A co-packer charges about ₹4 per cookie for mixing and freezing, so our cost is ₹27.50 instead of ₹23.50, and we keep ₹25.45 per kiosk cookie instead of ₹29.80. In return there is no kitchen to set up, no baker to manage, and no capacity limit as we grow. The real risks are quality and recipe leaks; the contract and quality system below are built for those.

How each number is built

  • Company kiosk: ₹60 less 5% GST is ₹57.14. Less ₹27.50 cost (co-packer dough ₹23.50, packaging ₹4) grossed up for 8% waste, and 3% payment fees, leaves ₹25.45. Monthly costs: rent ₹25,000, power ₹6,000, two salespeople ₹24,000 (legal minimum plus Puja bonus), rented oven and freezer ₹5,000, billing and CCTV ₹2,500, licences ₹1,500, marketing ₹15,000, repairs ₹3,000, dough delivery ₹3,000.
  • Cookie-jar partner: the partner buys at ₹40 plus GST and sells at ₹60, keeping a third. We pay ₹27.50 to make it, ₹1.50 to deliver, allow 10% for unsold cookies taken back, and pay our sales executive 5%.
  • Gift boxes: ₹330 a box of 6 (₹55 a cookie) less GST, cookie cost, ₹8 box, 1% fees and 5% commission. No waste, because boxes are made to order.
  • Franchise kiosk, our side: 6% royalty on sales after GST (₹3.43 a cookie) plus ₹2.50 margin on each dough and packaging kit we supply at ₹30.

The franchisee's side

A franchise only sells if it pays the franchisee well. They invest ₹6–8 lakh including a ₹2.5 lakh fee. After paying ₹30 a kit, 6% royalty and 2% to the marketing fund, they keep ₹18.16 a cookie. With costs of ₹75,000 a month, they break even at 138 cookies a day, make about ₹45,000 a month at 220 a day and ₹61,000 at 250, and earn back their investment in 11 to 16 months. That is the pitch, and it has to be proven at company kiosks first.

Growth roadmap

Each phase ends with a gate. The next phase starts only when every condition in the gate is met, however tempting an opportunity looks.

Phase 0
Months −3 to 0
Build
Set up the company and the supply. Register a private limited company, file the trademark, finish the recipe with a co-packer, sign the kiosk lease, get the licences, hire the store lead.
  • Owner's time: about 15 hours a week. This is a project, not a job.
  • Gate: 3 trial batches from the co-packer pass the quality spec; licences in hand.
Phase 1
Months 0–6
Prove
One company kiosk, 10 partners, delivery apps, WhatsApp orders. Choose a high-footfall site such as a mall, a college area or Sevoke Road. A part-time sales executive signs café and gym partners.
  • Targets: kiosk at 250 cookies a day by month 6; 10 partners reordering every week; 4.5+ Google rating.
  • Gate: kiosk above 200 a day for 3 months, waste under 8%, co-packer passing 98% of quality checks.
Phase 2
Months 6–18
Repeat
Kiosks 2 and 3, 40 partners, gifting, an operations head. Open each new kiosk in a different format (mall, college zone, near NJP station or the bus terminus) to learn which works best. Hire the operations head between months 6 and 9; the owner moves to a chair role. Qualify a second co-packer. Write the franchise manual.
  • Targets: 3 kiosks averaging 230 a day; 40 partners; 1,500 gift-box cookies a month, with 3 to 5 times that around Diwali.
  • Gate: all 3 kiosks profitable for 6 months, one of them run by a team hired after launch; trademark filed; franchise agreement drafted by a lawyer.
Phase 3
Months 18–36
Franchise
15 franchise kiosks across North Bengal and Sikkim. Two pilot franchises in Siliguri first, then Jalpaiguri, Cooch Behar, Alipurduar, Malda, Darjeeling, Kalimpong and Gangtok. 100 cookie-jar partners, including hotels and homestays in the hills. Packaged gift boxes in partner shops once shelf life is tested.
  • Targets: 1 new franchise a month after the pilots; 5,500 cookies a day across the network.
  • Decision point: at this volume, either sign a second co-packer or build a company dough facility paid for by the dough margin.
Phase 4
Years 3–5
Region
Guwahati and the Northeast through a master franchisee, who opens and manages outlets in their region. Online gift boxes across India. The owner's job is now choosing partners and protecting the brand.

Choosing the co-packer

  • FSSAI licence covering bakery manufacturing
  • Spare capacity of at least 2,000 dough balls a day
  • Blast or deep freezer and clean, separate production area
  • Within 30 minutes of the first kiosk
  • Willing to sign a confidentiality agreement and allow visits without notice
  • Does not sell its own cookies under another brand

What the contract must say

  • The recipe belongs to the company and stays confidential, with a penalty for misuse
  • Specification: 75 g ± 3 g, named ingredient brands, labelled batch codes
  • Price per ball linked to butter and chocolate prices, reviewed every quarter
  • Order cut-off 21:00, delivery by 7:00
  • A sample from every batch kept for 7 days
  • Right to audit, and to end the contract on 30 days' notice

Protect the recipe further by buying the signature chocolate and any flavourings yourself and supplying them to the co-packer. Qualify a second co-packer by month 12, so no single supplier can stop the business.

Outlet operations

Every kiosk, company or franchise, runs the same routine from the Operations Playbook: opening and closing checklists, hourly bake plan, waste logging, one UPI QR on the bill, blind cash count. Each kiosk keeps 2 days of dough in its freezer so a missed delivery never closes it. A board at the counter shows the time of the next fresh batch.

Technology

  • Billing app with multi-outlet and stock (for example Petpooja): every company and franchise outlet must use it. Royalty is calculated from it, and the dough order is generated from it.
  • Simple CRM (Zoho Bigin or a shared Google Sheet) for partner, corporate and franchise leads.
  • WhatsApp Business for orders, the broadcast list and alerts.
  • Payroll and attendance app (PagarBook, Kredily) with selfie attendance at the outlet.
  • Accounting (Zoho Books or Tally), kept by the CA.
  • One dashboard (Google Looker Studio, free) showing every outlet's sales, waste, rating and dough gap, sent to the owner every Monday.

Who makes a good franchisee

A local investor with ₹8–10 lakh available, who lives in the town, will be at the outlet daily or employ a full-time manager, and has run a shop or food business before. Turn down investors who want a passive investment with no one on site.

Process

  1. Enquiry form and a 20-minute call.
  2. Discovery day at a company kiosk: work a shift.
  3. Site proposed by the franchisee, checked against our site rules.
  4. Agreement signed, fee paid.
  5. 30 days of setup with the company's fit-out vendors; 7 days of training.
  6. Launch with the company's launch kit and team for the first week.

India has no single franchise law, so the agreement does all the work. Have a franchise lawyer draft it and register the trademark before signing the first franchisee.
