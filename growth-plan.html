Growth plan · Siliguri, West Bengal

Cookie Shop Growth Plan

How one cookie store with a central dough kitchen, two salespeople and the owner grows into five stores and beyond. The kitchen makes all the dough. Stores only bake and sell. Each new store is cheaper to open and easier to run than the last.

Built on the figures in the Cookie Shop Profit Planner (100 g cookie, ₹30 cost, ₹60 average price, 5% GST, 8% waste) and the Operations Playbook. Rents, salaries and sales per store are estimates; replace them with real numbers as you go.

The model

This is a hub-and-spoke business. One central kitchen (the hub) makes, scoops and freezes dough. Every store (a spoke) gets frozen dough each morning and bakes it fresh through the day. Customers get the same cookie at every store, and store staff need only one skill: baking from frozen and selling.

flowchart LR
  S[Suppliers: butter, flour, chocolate] --> K[Central kitchen]
  K -->|frozen dough, every morning| A[Store 1]
  K --> B[Store 2]
  K --> C[Mall kiosk]
  K -->|baked in kitchen| D[Swiggy and Zomato orders]
  K --> E[Cafes and hotels]
  A --> R[Billing app: sales and stock]
  B --> R
  C --> R
  R -->|tomorrow's dough order| K

Why this grows well:

  1. Same taste everywhere. One team mixes every batch, so a recipe can't drift from store to store.
  2. Smaller, cheaper stores. Without a mixer, wash area and dough storage, a store needs 100 to 200 sq ft, so a kiosk or a small counter works.
  3. Easier hiring. Salespeople learn to bake from frozen in a day. Only the kitchen needs a skilled baker.
  4. Better buying. One kitchen buys butter and chocolate for every store, so you get bulk prices. The plan assumes cost per cookie falls from ₹30 to ₹28 at three stores and ₹27 at five.

At one store, the separate kitchen costs you money. It adds about ₹32,500 a month in rent, power, delivery and equipment payments, so profit at 250 cookies a day drops from about ₹76,500 to about ₹57,500, and break-even rises from 138 to 166 cookies a day. It pays off from the second store. To keep that cost low at the start, you can rent a first store with a back room and use the back room as the kitchen until store 2 opens.

Two salespeople at ₹10,000 each is below the West Bengal minimum wage of ₹10,558 a month for unskilled shop staff in city areas (from July 2026). Paying the legal minimum adds about ₹1,100 per store per month, plus Puja bonus.

When to open the next store

Opening too early is the most common way small chains fail: the new store drains cash and the owner's attention before the first one is stable. Open the next store only when every box below is true.

The four stages

Each stage changes what you, the owner, spend your time on. Growth only works if you hand off the old job before taking on the new one.

Stage 1
Months 0–9
One store and the kitchen. You make dough in the kitchen each morning and deliver it. Two salespeople run the store in two shifts.
  • Your job: make dough, fix problems, write down every recipe and routine.
  • Build: the playbook systems, billing app with stock transfer from kitchen to store, nightly report.
  • Done when: 3 profitable months in a row and every gate above passes.
Stage 2
Months 9–15
Hire a baker; open store 2. Train a kitchen baker for one month before store 2 opens, so you leave the kitchen first. Store 2 goes in a different part of Siliguri with different crowds.
  • Your job: plan dough orders, check quality, visit both stores, find sites.
  • Build: the new-store kit, store-by-store profit report.
  • Done when: store 2 is above its own break-even (about 118 cookies a day) for 3 months.
Stage 3
Year 2
Stores 3 to 5 and delivery. Hire an operations manager who visits every store each day. Add delivery-app orders baked in the kitchen, and supply cafés and hotels from spare kitchen capacity.
  • Your job: money, suppliers, new sites, the operations manager.
  • Build: bulk buying contracts, a second freezer, a backup generator for the kitchen.
  • Done when: five stores, profit near ₹3 lakh a month, and a month passes with you in no store and not in the kitchen.
Stage 4
Year 3 onward
Beyond Siliguri. Nearby towns first: Jalpaiguri, then tourist towns such as Darjeeling, Kalimpong and Gangtok, where gift boxes sell well. Frozen dough travels a few hours in insulated boxes; for longer trips, use a refrigerated van or open a second kitchen.
  • Your job: brand, new towns, partners.
  • Option: franchising, but only after 5 company stores make money and the brand name is a registered trademark.

The central kitchen

Setting it up

  • Space: 300 to 500 sq ft on a back street, not the high street. Needs water, drainage, a wash area and a strong power supply.
  • Licences: the kitchen needs its own FSSAI licence as a manufacturing unit, and its own trade licence. Each store also needs its own FSSAI licence or registration and trade licence. Confirm with your CA.
  • Equipment, about ₹3 to 4 lakh: a 20-litre planetary mixer, two chest freezers, steel work tables, scales, scoops, food-grade crates, insulated boxes with ice packs, and an inverter or generator.
  • Capacity: a 20-litre mixer handles very roughly 6 to 8 kg of cookie dough a batch, which is 60 to 80 cookies of 100 g. Measure your real output in the first month. Past about 800 dough balls a day, add a second shift or a 40-litre mixer.

Two rules that stop most problems

  1. Every dough ball is counted. For each store, dough received must equal cookies sold plus waste plus dough left in the freezer. A gap of more than 1% is checked the same day.
  2. Every store keeps 2 days of dough in its own freezer. A power cut, a sick baker or a strike day at the kitchen then doesn't close any store. Use the oldest dough first, and set a use-by of 30 days from the batch date.

Keep stores 2 to 3 km apart and aim each at a different crowd: shoppers, students, office workers or travellers. Two stores on the same street mostly split the same customers.

New-store kit

Write this once, after store 1, and reuse it for every store. Opening store 5 should feel like repeating a checklist, not starting over.

  1. Site checklist: footfall count, rent limit (under 15% of expected sales), power load, signboard visibility, distance from your other stores.
  2. Fit-out pack: one layout drawing, one equipment list, the same counter, display and signboard from the same vendors.
  3. Licence list: FSSAI, trade licence and Shops and Establishments for the new address, applied for the day the rent agreement is signed.
  4. Hiring pack: job post, interview questions, paid trial shift. Hire 3 weeks before opening.
  5. Training: the 5-day plan, with days 2 to 5 done at an existing store.
  6. Billing setup: copy store 1's menu, recipes and alerts into the new outlet in the billing app.
  7. Launch: free samples for the first 3 days, Instagram posts, flyers to nearby colleges and offices.
