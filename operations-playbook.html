Operations playbook

Cookie Shop Operations Playbook

How a walk-in cookie store runs every day without the owner in it. Software records every sale, every stock movement and every checklist. Staff follow written steps. You read one report a night and spend about two hours a week on the business.

Targets match the example figures in the Cookie Shop Profit Planner: 400 cookies a day, break-even near 230. Change them once you have your own numbers.

Who does what

The shop has three layers. Each one only makes the decisions that belong to it.

Owner (you)

about 2 hrs / week
  • Read the nightly report (5 minutes)
  • Weekly review call with the manager
  • Approve spending above a set limit
  • Set prices, the menu and targets
  • Hire or fire the manager

Store manager

full time, salaried
  • Writes the staff rota
  • Approves supplier orders
  • Trains staff and checks their work
  • Handles complaints and refunds
  • Owns the numbers in section 6

Shift staff

2 per shift, hourly
  • Bake to the hourly plan
  • Serve customers and use the till
  • Complete opening and closing checklists
  • Log waste at the end of each day
  • One senior staff member acts as shift lead

Spending limit. The manager can spend up to a fixed amount per order without asking, for example 300 in your currency. Anything larger goes to you for approval in the purchasing app. This one rule removes most of the day-to-day calls to the owner.

How data flows

Each sale updates stock, reports and accounts with no one typing it twice. People only step in where something needs judgement.

Recipes and baking plan

Anyone trained for five days should bake a cookie you cannot tell apart from yours. That needs recipes by weight, not by cups, and a fixed plan for how much to bake each hour.

Recipe card format

CLASSIC CHOC CHIP Batch: 48 cookies at 60 g dough each ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────── Butter, unsalted, soft 680 g Mix 3 min with sugars Brown sugar 540 g White sugar 270 g Eggs 4 (200 g) Add one at a time Vanilla 15 g Flour 900 g Mix on low until just combined Baking soda 10 g Salt 12 g Chocolate chunks 720 g Fold in by hand ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────── Scoop 60 g · Chill 24 h minimum · Bake 180 °C, 11 min Done when: edges golden, centre still soft Photo of correct cookie: pinned above the oven

Make dough the day before and freeze scooped balls. Staff then only bake, which is faster and more consistent.

Stopping leaks

When the owner is away, money leaks through cash, free cookies, missing stock and extra hours. Each of these checks takes no one's time day to day.

  1. Every sale goes through the till. The display shows prices; the customer screen shows the total. A sign at the counter says customers get a free cookie if they are not offered a receipt, so customers check for you.
  2. Each staff member has their own till login. Refunds, voids and discounts carry a name.
  3. Blind cash count. Staff count the cash before the till tells them what it should be.
  4. Weekly stock count of the five costliest items (butter, chocolate, nuts, boxes, eggs). The till compares actual stock with what sales say should be left.
  5. Clock-in only at the shop, by location or a tablet at the counter, so hours can't be padded.
  6. Staff treats are logged as waste with a reason. Allow a fixed amount per shift, so no one needs to hide it.
  7. Camera on the till and the back door, reviewed only when a number looks wrong.

Hiring and training

Hiring

Hire for reliability and friendliness; baking can be taught. Keep the job post, interview questions and a paid two-hour trial shift the same every time, so the manager can run hiring without you.

Manager pay

Pay a base salary plus a monthly bonus of 10% of profit above break-even. This rewards selling more and wasting less. Paying on sales alone would reward overstaffing and discounting.

90 days to step back

You cannot be absent on day one. Build the system while you are still in the shop, then hand it over in stages.

Before opening
Set up the till, stock items and recipes in it. Write recipe cards and checklists. Hire the manager first and have them help hire staff.
  • Done when: a test day runs with every sale, clock-in and checklist recorded.
Days 1–30
You work in the shop alongside the manager. Fix every step that confuses staff. Turn on the nightly report and alerts.
  • Done when: the manager runs a full day while you only watch.
Days 31–60
You are in the shop two days a week. The manager writes the rota, approves orders and trains new staff. Set the hourly baking plan from real sales.
  • Done when: two weeks with no red numbers and no calls to you.
Days 61–90
You stop scheduled visits. Run only the nightly report, the Monday call and the monthly review. Start the manager bonus.
  • Done when: a month closes at or above your target profit without you in the shop.
